Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Sherman

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Wood flooring on the first level that has begun to curl up along the seams
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.
