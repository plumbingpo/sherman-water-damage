Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Sherman

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

Our trade is water damage restoration, and roofing is a separate one. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Document What You SeeTake photos and video of the ceiling, the attic and any damaged belongings before you move or throw anything away, then call us to start drying.
Our Process

How We Handle Roof Leak Water Damage

Map Where Water Went

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks wall cavities, ceilings and the attic and charts the whole route the water took, even where the room below shows nothing.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Shingles that are cracked, curled or blown off by wind, exposing the underlayment.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Wall Cavities

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Attic Insulation

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How much attic insulation is wet and whether it has to be removed.
  • The amount of ceiling and wall drywall that needs to be cut out.
  • How easy the attic is to reach, how high the ceilings are, and if the roof needs a tarp before work begins.
  • Finish work, including matching an existing texture and painting the room.
