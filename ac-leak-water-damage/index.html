Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Sherman

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Closet Walls and Platform

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Insulation Around the Unit

Insulation around an attic unit soaks up overflow and holds it against the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

What We Do

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, usually 3 to 5, while we track the readings. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.

Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How much flooring, wall and ceiling material needs replacement.
  • Whether the water is still clean or has to be handled as Category 2.
  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
