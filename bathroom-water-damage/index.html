Bathroom Water Damage Help in Sherman

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Sherman, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

Poor Ventilation

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
