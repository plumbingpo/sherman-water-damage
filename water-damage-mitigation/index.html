Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. In Sherman, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Done well, it shrinks the repair that follows and sometimes removes the need for one.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. In fact there are two separate stages. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Sherman Restoration:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. Should a lasting fix require a plumber or a roofer, our team coordinates when that happens.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize the Structure

We remove what cannot be saved, treat surfaces with antimicrobial, and place dehumidifiers and air movers. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Slope the soil away from the house so rainwater does not pool against the walls.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • The leak is fixed, yet cabinets, walls or flooring remain wet.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
